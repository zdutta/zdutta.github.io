**Author:** Rory Sutherland

## Reflection

> "The opposite of a good idea can also be a good idea."

Sutherland's argument is that we over-trust logic because it is easy to defend in a meeting, and under-value psychology because it is not. A spreadsheet answer can be wrong and still get approved. A strange answer can be right and never get proposed.

His Eurostar example makes it concrete. Engineers wanted billions to cut the London-Paris journey by forty minutes. Spend a fraction of that on making the ride itself better, he argues, and passengers would ask for the train to be slowed down. Same problem, entirely different framing of what "value" means.

The useful takeaway is not "be irrational." It is that perceived value is real value, and the people who only optimize the measurable parts leave most of the opportunity on the table.
